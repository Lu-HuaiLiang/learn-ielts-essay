**A1．每个人都能创作艺术吗？｜296词**

**题目（讲义原文）**

> Some people say that artworks (e.g. painting, music, poetry) can be created by everybody, whereas others say they can only be made by those with special ability.
>
> Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

**小修版**

The importance of **art is beyond doubt;** however, opinions differ as to **who can create** artworks (e.g. painting, music, poetry). Some believe that they **can** be made by every individual, whereas others argue they can only be produced by people with certain skills and abilities. **I believe anyone can create art for self-expression, although producing widely acclaimed work generally requires greater skill.**

The primary justification for everyone’s artistic potential resides in the very nature of art. Art is a channel for people to express their unique ideas and experiences through various art mediums, ranging from poems to paintings. For example, **drawing** is a popular art **form** with a low **barrier to entry,** and as long as creators have a pen and a piece of paper, no matter which culture or generation they belong to, they **can create** art **freely.**

Others, however, highlight that artworks should be distinguished by their high quality. **Creations** such as poems and **musical compositions that are widely recognised as significant works of art usually demonstrate a high level of technical skill or a perceptive understanding of social phenomena.** Although everyone can try to create a **work of art, a widely acclaimed** piece generally requires more **developed** skills and abilities.

I think opinions vary according to the definition of art. Although art per se should be regarded as a way to represent things or express ideas, the artworks that people usually talk about **are often those recognised for their** artistic value **and, sometimes, their** commercial value, behind which are the talent and **extensive** training of the artists. **This helps to explain why widely acclaimed** artworks **are often** made by professionals, be they musicians or writers. All can create art in ways that they want; **not all** can create art that **gains wide recognition.**

**最值得理解的三处调整**

1．先区分“能参与创作”与“作品获得广泛认可”。原来的 a truly generally recognized product 和 All …; only a few … 容易把“他人喜欢”说成少数人才做得到；改为 widely acclaimed / gains wide recognition，保留平行对照，也使标准更清楚。开头直接给出这一立场。

2．让例子与工具一致：a pen and a piece of paper 更直接支持 drawing，而非 painting。把 conducting the art creation 改为 create art freely，把 high levels of techniques 改为 a high level of technical skill；原有的长让步结构和 a channel for … 都保留。

3．can recognized 本身缺少被动结构；修订后的整句用 those recognised for … 表示“因……受到认可”。原来的 That justified the status quo 改为 This helps to explain why：专业人士较常创作出名作，可以解释现象，却不能证明只有专业人士能创作。art mediums、art per se 和 be they musicians or writers 本来可用，无须为了小修全部替换。

**A2．艺术能告诉我们哪些科学无法充分传达的东西？｜332词**

**题目（讲义原文）**

> In today’s world of advanced science and technology, many still greatly value artists such as musicians, painters and writers.
>
> What can the arts tell us about life that science cannot?

**小修版**

The importance of science and **technology is beyond doubt;** however, many people still value the arts despite a growing emphasis on science and technology. This occurs for several reasons, foremost of which is the fact that **the** arts **convey human experience** and aesthetic **values in ways that scientific explanation alone cannot fully capture.**

The underlying reason for the **importance** of **the** arts is that they allow for self-expression, be it for individuals or society. For individuals, the ability to express themselves is a crucial element of being fully human and **maintaining** mental **well-being. Through** painting, music, and **dance, people can express their thoughts and feelings and help others understand what experiences such as joy or loss feel like from the inside. For society** as a **whole,** artworks can partly **reflect shared values** and enrich **cultural life.** When people visit museums or art galleries, they appreciate paintings or sculptures created by artists so that they can gain a better understanding of the past, ranging from what **gives** one period its **distinctive character** to how values and morality **have** changed. The same principle also applies to stories written by writers and music by musicians.

In addition, art **reveals the aesthetic value of everyday life,** while science **helps to explain how things function.** Although science is capable of making tangible improvements in the lives of individuals, **technical function alone does not determine what people find beautiful or meaningful.** For example, the **appeal** of **a smartphone may rest** ultimately not only on its **operating** system but also on its industrial design. Science and technology **provide** the system **itself,** but art **helps to shape its visual appearance, showing** how **people seek beauty even in objects** designed **for practical use.**

In conclusion, the arts still occupy a prominent position in society as they **convey personal feelings, shared cultural experiences** and aesthetic **values. These perspectives complement scientific knowledge, which helps to explain the world but does not by itself fully communicate what it feels like to live** in **it.**

**最值得理解的三处调整**

1．把“艺术受欢迎的原因”拉回题目所问的“艺术告诉我们什么”。self-expression 后补出读者能获得的理解：what experiences such as joy or loss feel like from the inside。这样表达、作品、受众的理解形成一条因果链，而不只是说上艺术课有助放松。

2．修好词性与搭配，但保留对照句。原文 art brings aesthetics while science brings functional 中 functional 是形容词，不能这样作宾语；修订用 aesthetic value 与 how things function 分别说明艺术和科学的侧重点，并用 alone 避免断言科学与审美毫无关联。unique characters 改为 distinctive character，表示一个时期的整体特征。

3．原稿把乔布斯上书法课与 iPhone 的大小写命名直接联系起来，材料没有支持这条因果。这里保留“手机技术＋工业设计”的例子，改成一般性的 may rest … not only … but also …；结尾也直接总结情感、文化经验、审美价值，替换未在前文论证过的“维持现状”。

**A3．艺术经费是否应转投公共服务？｜402词**

**题目（讲义原文）**

> Some people say arts such as music and painting cannot directly improve the quality of people’s life, so the government shouldn’t put money on art such as music and painting, instead, they should spend more money on construction of public services. Do you agree or disagree?

**小修版**

There is a fierce debate about how governments allocate budgets. It is proposed that the money allocated to art (e.g., music and painting) should be channelled into **improving** public services. Although **essential needs for** such **services** should be met, **I disagree that arts funding should be withdrawn, as** other considerations justify **continued investment in** the **arts.**

The argument in favour of public services is that people can directly benefit from such investment. Many cities’ public services are **inadequate;** for example, **accessible** public **facilities may be damaged** or ill-maintained in some cities, causing a lot of inconvenience to **people with disabilities.** If more money were to be allocated to public services, this would allow **them** to live **more independently** and travel **with fewer barriers,** which is an important element **of** a humane and civilized society **serving** all rather than most. **Those** people **could** benefit **directly** from such **improvements, which may be more urgent than additional spending on** music or painting.

However, it does not necessarily mean that there is no justification for **investment in** the **arts.** For individuals, the ability to express themselves is a crucial element of being fully human and **maintaining** mental **well-being.** This explains the popularity of classes for painting, music, and dance as a means of relaxing and expressing one’s thoughts and feelings. For society, artwork can partly **reflect shared values** and enrich **cultural life.** When people visit museums or art galleries, they appreciate paintings or sculptures created by artists so that they can gain a better understanding of the past, ranging from what **gives** one period its **distinctive character** to how values and morality **have** changed.

Moreover, it is worthwhile to point out that **once essential public service needs have been met, allocating all arts funding to further service improvements may bring only limited additional benefits. This reflects** the **principle** of diminishing **returns: beyond a certain point, each additional unit of** investment in a particular area **may produce** a **smaller additional benefit. In such circumstances, transferring a modest arts budget may make little difference** to public **services;** by contrast, **retaining it could preserve valuable opportunities for artistic participation** and **appreciation.**

In conclusion, even though **urgent needs** for public services should be satisfied, **continued investment in** the **arts** is **justified** in view of its **cultural and personal benefits. Budget allocation** should be **guided by** a **clear** set of **criteria that balances essential service needs with opportunities** for **cultural participation.**

**最值得理解的三处调整**

1．统一“经费”类搭配：art construction 改为 investment in the arts / arts funding；construction of public services 改为 improving public services。服务不一定涉及建造，艺术也不宜机械翻译为“艺术建设”。开头明确：支持满足基本公共服务需求，同时反对撤掉艺术经费。

2．把“残障者几乎无法从艺术受益”改为“无障碍设施可能更紧迫”。live and travel as normal people 也改为 live more independently and travel with fewer barriers。这样比较的是公共支出的优先程度，而不是断言某一群体没有审美或文化需求。

3．收益递减必须有条件。原文从“公共服务预算很大”直接推到“追加投入收益很小”，中间缺了一步；改为 once essential public service needs have been met，再讨论新增投入的额外收益。结尾据此要求平衡基本需求与文化参与，不再假定现实中的预算配置已经合理。

**B1．老建筑修复经费是否应转投道路？｜411词**

**题目（讲义原文）**

> The restoration of old buildings in major cities in the world costs numerous government's expenditures. This money should be used for road development. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

**小修版**

There has been a fierce yet unresolved debate as to how the government **allocates its** budget. It is proposed that the money allocated to the renovation and preservation of old buildings should be channelled into road development. Although **the demand for road development** should be met, other considerations justify the policies adopted to preserve and restore **these buildings.**

The argument in favour of road construction is that it can greatly contribute to city dwellers’ well-being. Many commuters are negatively **affected** by traffic, mentally and physically, especially the heavy traffic during rush hours. If more money were to be allocated to road development, ranging from the construction of flyovers to the **removal** of **traffic** bottlenecks, fewer commuters **might** complain about the experience of getting stuck in traffic for hours. **It** seems, therefore, that citizens can benefit from such reasonable budget allocation.

However, it does not necessarily mean that there is no justification for the preservation of old buildings. **Many of these** buildings reflect the beauty of local culture and heritage. **Would** future **generations** recognize their cultural identity **as readily** if much of the physical evidence were to be erased? **Probably** not if all these old buildings disappeared and only anonymous housing estates **remained.** In addition, restored buildings can act as an economic engine, drawing tourists from all over the world. **A city,** for example, **could preserve an old** temple, capitalizing on its cultural and historic flavour to attract tourists. Therefore, the attempt to maintain such old buildings is **justifiable.**

Moreover, it is worthwhile to point out that if the money allocated to maintaining old buildings were to be spent on other important things (e.g., road development), the situation **might** improve **little where road infrastructure is already well developed.** This **can be explained by** the **principle** of diminishing **returns,** which **means** that as investment in a particular area increases, the **additional benefit from each unit** of **investment may decline** after a certain **point.** In **such circumstances,** the proposal **may bring only limited improvements;** by contrast, governments and citizens **can** benefit culturally from old **buildings even when their preservation is** supported by a **relatively** small proportion of the government budget.

In conclusion, even though the high demand for road development should be satisfied, the preservation of old buildings is indispensable in view of their cultural and economic importance. **Funding for preservation** should **therefore** be maintained, **with** budget **allocations** carefully made according to a strict set of **criteria that balances transport needs with heritage protection.**

**最值得理解的三处调整**

1．保留“道路收益→文化与旅游价值→预算取舍”的推进。统一 government allocates its budget 的机构指代，并把 Will future generation … if … were … 改为 Would future generations … if … were …。英式英语中的 government 配复数动词并非一概错误，这里主要是统一指代；道路的瓶颈则用 removal of traffic bottlenecks 表达得更自然。

2．把未经核实的成都寺庙案例改为城市保留古寺的假设，继续保留 capitalizing on its cultural and historic flavour 这一原有表达。

3．为 diminishing returns 补上道路设施已较完善的适用条件，避免由边际收益下降直接推出所有新增投入几乎无效；结尾改成预算应如何分配，不再假定现行预算已经最优。

**题目（讲义原文）**

**小修版**

**One** of the worrying trends in modern society is that people do **not get enough** regular **exercise.** Some policymakers propose that all towns and cities should allocate funds to build more outdoor areas, such as squares and parks. Although this proposal can seemingly mitigate public health **concerns,** it is **not necessarily financially viable or effective in every town or city.**

The argument in favour of this proposal is that it can encourage citizens to exercise. **Today’s** sedentary lifestyle and stressful working conditions **can** mean that physical activity is no longer **a regular** part of either our work or our leisure time; this problem is **exacerbated where** insufficient public outdoor activity space is provided. If the policy **of increasing park space** were to be adopted, the new outdoor public places would offer people **more** space to do some physical **activities (e.g.,** jogging and taking a walk), thus **helping to mitigate** the problem of declining public health. It seems sensible, therefore, to adopt this **proposal where such facilities are lacking.**

However, the effort to enable all towns and cities to build more public spaces **may be difficult** to **sustain,** as it presumes that all governments have extra money to implement this policy. Governments have to budget carefully to meet **demands** from every field, ranging from healthcare services and public transport to the state pension system, which means **they may have limited scope to increase** the amount of money allocated to public **facilities.** For some **less affluent** regions, large outdoor spaces require construction expenses and **ongoing** maintenance costs (e.g., waste management), which **may mean cuts to spending in** other fields.

Moreover, **it** is not **simply** the lack of outdoor activity space but **also a** heavy workload or study burden that **can discourage** people from exercising regularly. Therefore, the worrying situation **may continue if** people **still lack time** to go to parks or squares to take a walk. The **decline in** public health could be **addressed** by helping people realize the importance of exercising **regularly and** making **time for** it **in work and study schedules, prompting** people to make **full** use of existing outdoor activity space to exercise.

Although declining public health warrants attention, it is not **necessarily** financially viable for all cities and towns to build more outdoor activity spaces. This proposal **may** have little effect **where lack of time remains the main barrier,** and thus success **in** improving public health **also** rests on improved health awareness **and opportunities to exercise,** rather than extra facilities and **spaces alone.**

**最值得理解的三处调整**

3．工作与学业负担和空间不足可以同时影响运动，因此保留原强调句并加入 not simply… but also…；后文补上安排运动时间，使建议回应前面的时间障碍。

The argument in favour of this policy is that it can greatly reduce the time **spent** commuting. **Commuters** with a limited **budget may** choose a residential area on the periphery of the city, which means they have to get up early, catch the early bus or train, and arrive at their **workplaces** on time. **If** the proposal were to be adopted, more **apartments** would **become available** to those **commuters; provided these homes were affordable and close to their workplaces, this could allow** them to **spend less time travelling and** have more time to sleep and relax. Moreover, the distance from the company to the home can be reduced by relocating some companies to the inner **suburbs, provided this brings them closer to where their employees live.** Replacing parks and gardens is unacceptable due to their importance to **citizens as places for recreation and exercise;** by contrast, it **may be** more viable for some companies or factories to relocate **where commercial rents are lower in** the suburbs than in city **centres** and **suitable** land is **available without sacrificing public green spaces.**

In conclusion, the proposal to **replace parks and gardens with** residential areas is **not the best solution,** in view of the **other causes of long commuting times and the value of public green spaces.** Other sensible **programmes,** such as improving road conditions and **relocating** companies, should be **considered where appropriate** to reduce the time spent **commuting without sacrificing those spaces.**

**题目（讲义原文）**

> Children can learn effectively from watching television. Therefore children should be encouraged to watch television regularly both at home and at school. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

**小修版**

Television has grown significantly in educational importance; therefore, with such benefits comes a proposal that watching television should be a **regular** part of children’s lives, be it at home or school. Despite the tangible benefits, watching TV regularly at school **can be counterproductive if it replaces teacher-led instruction,** while it can be allowed at home with proper supervision.

The argument in favour of TV-watching activities at home and school is that **they** can **maximise** the educational benefits that accrue to children. **这批文章最值得继续沿用的写法**

① 让步之后限制推论：Although … / However, this does not necessarily mean that …。承认一个方案有价值，再判断它是否足以支持“更多、全部、强制、最佳”这些更强的要求。

② 长句保留清楚的中间步骤：条件或原因 → 人的行为／可获得的资源发生什么变化 → 最终结果。长句中的 which、this、such 必须有明确指向。

③ 用有条件的结论提高准确性：provided that、where、once、when 和 may / can 不是单纯把语气写弱，而是交代一个判断在什么情况下成立。

④ 保留熟悉的正式结构：The primary justification … resides in …、With … comes …、be they …、the first being …。这些结构本身并没有错；修改重点是其后接的内容是否准确、是否真正推进论证。

⑤ 结尾回到原题中的具体要求：艺术资金是否撤掉、公园 是否应更多、四周年假是否合理、强制服务是否弊大于利。避免用 The status quo should be maintained 代替尚未说明的具体判断。
